Proposed for the Parish Council

Parish Directory

Knowing one another by name is a good deal of how a parish works. It is how a newcomer stops being a stranger, and how somebody notices that a family has not been in for a fortnight. There is no parish directory here to open, and nothing on this page creates one. It sets out what a directory could be, for the Parish Council to consider.

A proposal, not a plan

What a parish directory could be

Set out here so the Parish Council has something specific to say yes or no to.

Proposed — no directory exists yet

What it would be

A list of the households of this parish, kept for this parish. Being in it would be a choice, made household by household — nobody added because they are on a roll somewhere, and nobody obliged to explain why they would rather not be listed. Each household would decide what of its own entry it wants shown, and would be able to change that, or come out of the directory altogether, whenever it likes. A decision made in one year is not a decision anyone is held to in the next.

Who could see it

Parish members the parish has approved, one at a time, and nobody else. Not the public, not a search engine, and not this demo site. A directory is only worth having if the people in it are confident about who is looking at it, so the answer to that question has to be a short and boring one.

Where it would live

In an established parish-management system, chosen by the parish — the kind of thing many parishes already run, maintained by people whose work it is. Not something written into this website. This site would only ever hold the doorway: a page that explains what the directory is and sends an approved member to sign in. The details themselves would never pass through here.

What it would never be

There would be no shared parish password, because a password everybody knows is a password nobody has. There would be no member details on a public page, at any point, for any reason — not a partial list, not a list with the telephone numbers taken out. If the choice ever came down to publishing something convenient or publishing nothing, it would be nothing.

What a household could choose to share

These are possibilities rather than fields that exist anywhere. They are here to show what a directory is for: it is not an address book so much as a way of knowing who is standing next to you.

  • Name and household Who is at home, written the way the household would write it.
  • Patron saint and name day So that a name day can be marked by the people who would want to mark it.
  • Ministries Which parts of parish life a household takes part in, for anyone wondering whom to ask.
  • Something they could teach The bread, the music, the language, the cooking before a feast — a skill or parish tradition they could teach, offered to whoever would like to learn it.
  • What to ask them about The one thing a person is always glad to be asked about, which is the hardest thing to guess and the easiest to offer.

Every line on this page is an idea for the Parish Council to weigh, not a decision anybody has taken. Whether the parish wants a directory at all, which system would hold it, and who would approve a member are all questions for the Council and Fr. John.

Until there is one, ask the office

The parish office is the way to reach anyone here. If you are trying to find a household, get a message to a ministry, or put a question to somebody whose name you have and whose number you have not, the office will pass it on or put you in touch.

This draft does not know whether the parish already keeps a directory of its own, printed or otherwise, or how a household would be listed in one. That is a question for the office rather than for this page.

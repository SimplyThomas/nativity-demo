Living the Traditions

Learning and passing on the traditions of Orthodox Christian life.

Some traditions are learned from books. Others are learned by standing beside someone who has been doing them for fifty years.

Proposed — an idea, not a schedule

Orthodox life is learned not only through books, but through prayer, worship, meals, feasts, fasting, hospitality, and traditions handed from one generation to the next.

Many people in our parish already know how to prepare the foods, fold the palms, bake the breads, sing the hymns, and keep the customs that have shaped Orthodox Christian life for generations.

Others would love to learn.

Living the Traditions is an idea for a series of hands-on parish gatherings where we can learn from one another, preserve what has been handed down to us, and help the next generation carry it forward.

Please teach us. We want to know how you did this. We want to carry it forward.

Teach

Do you know a tradition you could share?

Some of the most valuable knowledge in a parish is carried quietly by people who have been doing something for decades.

If you know how to make koliva, fold palm crosses, bake a traditional feast-day food, prepare something for Holy Week, sing a hymn, sew or embroider church items, or keep another Orthodox tradition, we would love to know.

You do not need to be an expert, and you do not need to run a class on your own. Standing at a table for twenty minutes and showing four people how you do it is a real contribution.

Passing it on

Living the Traditions is especially meant to create opportunities for the generations within our parish to learn from one another.

A recipe, a hymn, a prayer, a folding technique, a feast-day custom or a family tradition may seem ordinary to the person who has practised it for decades.

To someone who never had anyone to teach them, it can be a gift.

What one generation learned at home, the next generation may need to learn at church.

Workshops, and where a priest is needed

Some of these gatherings are practical workshops rather than formal catechesis. Learning to fold a palm cross or to shape a Lazarakia needs a steady pair of hands and someone patient beside you.

Where a topic touches doctrine, sacramental practice, liturgical practice, pastoral guidance, or anything else that calls for authoritative teaching, Father would approve the material and lead that part of it. A parishioner who knows how something is done is not thereby a teacher of the faith, and this page does not present anyone as one.

Nothing on this page has been reviewed or approved yet. It is a proposal for the Parish Council and for Fr. John.

The traditions of the Church are not museum pieces.

They are prayed, baked, sung, folded, cooked, carried, celebrated, and handed from person to person.

Help us learn what our parish would like to preserve and pass on.

Learn it. Live it. Pass it on.

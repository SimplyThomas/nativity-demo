Welcome
“Welcome one another, therefore, just as Christ has welcomed you, for the glory of God.”
Romans 15:7

For nearly two thousand years, the Orthodox Church has proclaimed the apostolic faith and gathered around the Eucharist in worship. That same life of prayer continues here in Fredericksburg.

If you are encountering Orthodoxy for the first time, you are welcome to visit. You do not need to know the prayers, understand when to stand, or know what to do beforehand. Simply come. We would be glad to have you with us.

Your First Sunday

Visiting an Orthodox church for the first time can feel unfamiliar. You don't need to know the prayers, when to stand, or what to do before you arrive. Someone will be there to welcome you, help you find your way, and give you a complimentary prayer book so you can follow along if you would like.

A practical note

When should I arrive?

If you would like to experience the full Sunday morning cycle of worship, join us for Orthros at 9:00 AM. If this is your first visit—especially if you are bringing young children—you are also welcome to arrive closer to the beginning of Divine Liturgy, which typically begins at 10:00 AM.

Orthros

The morning prayer service

Orthros, also called Matins, is the Church's morning service of prayer and preparation before the Divine Liturgy. It includes psalms, hymns, Scripture, and prayers that reflect the feast or saint being commemorated that day.

Visitors are welcome to attend Orthros, but they should not feel obligated to begin their first visit here. Many inquirers and families with young children choose to arrive closer to the beginning of the Divine Liturgy.

02
10:00 AM

Divine Liturgy

The principal worship service of Sunday

The Divine Liturgy is the central worship service of the Orthodox Church, when the parish gathers in prayer and celebrates the Holy Eucharist. Expect ancient prayers and hymns, readings from Scripture, incense, icons, and periods when the congregation stands.

Visitors are welcome to participate in the prayers and worship. Don't worry about knowing when to stand, sit, make the sign of the Cross, or venerate an icon. You are welcome to simply observe and pray.

03
Within the Liturgy

Holy Communion

A note for our visitors

The Eucharist is received by baptized and chrismated Orthodox Christians who are properly prepared to receive Holy Communion. If you are not Orthodox, please remain in place during Communion. This does not mean that you are unwelcome; Orthodox Christians understand Communion as an expression of full sacramental unity in the faith.

Visitors are welcome to receive antidoron, the blessed bread shared with the faithful and visitors. If you are unsure what to do at any point, simply ask the person who greeted you.

04
After Liturgy

The Agape Meal

Please stay and eat with us.

Our Sunday gathering doesn't end when the service does. After Divine Liturgy, we gather for our Agape Meal—a shared, potluck-style meal in the parish hall.

Visitors are warmly invited to join us. You do not need to bring anything. This is a wonderful opportunity to meet parishioners, ask questions, and get to know our community.

Questions people ask before coming

What should I wear?

Modest and reasonably neat. Many wear what they'd wear to a nice dinner. Shorts and jeans appear too. Nobody is checking.

Do I need to be Greek?

No. A growing share of our parish is not, including converts and families from other Orthodox traditions. The services are mostly in English.

Is there somewhere to sit?

Yes — full pews. Orthodox tradition stands for much of the service, but sit whenever you need to. Follow those around you or don't.

What about my children?

Bring them. Orthodox services are noisy at the edges and always have been. There is a cry room if you want one, but no obligation to use it.

Will I be singled out?

No. Visitors are never asked to stand or introduce themselves. Come, watch, leave whenever you like.

I'm interested in becoming Orthodox.

Speak with Fr. John — no commitment implied. Inquirers' conversations happen quietly and at your own pace, often over many months. If you would rather begin by watching, this video series explains the Divine Liturgy and the faith behind it.

Our Faith

The Church of the Apostles, still here

Orthodox Christianity is not a denomination that broke away from something. It is the continuous life of the Church founded at Pentecost, holding the same faith, the same sacraments and the same worship across twenty centuries.

That continuity is what a visitor feels first: the hymns, the icons, the fasting seasons, the shape of the year. None of it is nostalgia. It is the ordinary practice of a Church that never stopped.

The pages below are the parish's introduction to that life. If you would rather ask a person than read, that is always the better option — Fr. John is glad to talk.

We are a parish of the Greek Orthodox Metropolis of New Jersey, serving Orthodox Christians across Fredericksburg, Spotsylvania, Stafford and King George.

The community began as a small group of families meeting where they could, and grew into the church that stands today on Spotswood Furnace Road. Its name — the Nativity of the Theotokos — marks the birth of the Mother of God, celebrated each September the eighth.

Today the parish is a mix of families with Greek roots going back generations, Orthodox Christians who moved to the area for work or the military, and converts who found their way here from other traditions. That mix is the ordinary condition of an American Orthodox parish, and it is a good one.

Fr. John serves as presiding priest, assisted by an elected Parish Council and a large number of parishioners who quietly keep everything running.

Stewardship

Giving, and what it pays for

Stewardship is not dues. It is the offering by which the parish exists: the priest's salary, the utilities, the Sunday School materials, the charity the Philoptochos gives away, and the building itself.

Visitors are never expected to give. If you are new, put nothing in the tray and enjoy the coffee.

Fredericksburg Greek Festival

Three days. Everyone invited.

Food, music, dancing and church tours on the grounds at Spotswood Furnace Road.

Eat

Souvlaki, pastitsio, spanakopita, gyros and a pastry table built over several weeks by the Philoptochos. Dine in or take away.

Watch

Live music and performances by the parish dance troupe through each afternoon and evening.

See

Guided tours of the church on the hour — the icons, the architecture, and what all of it means. No booking needed.

Volunteers make it happen

The festival is run entirely by parishioners and friends of the parish — cooking, serving, parking, setup and teardown. You do not need to be Orthodox or Greek to help, and many volunteers are neighbours who simply enjoy it.

Shifts are two hours. Sign-ups open in late summer.

Dates & tickets

Dates for this year are announced on the festival website, along with the menu and advance banquet tickets.

Rental is open to parishioners and to the wider Fredericksburg community. Rates differ for the two, and a refundable deposit is required.

Parish life

Fellowship & Care

Our life together does not end when the Divine Liturgy concludes. Coffee hour, shared meals, feast-day celebrations and caring for one another in times of need are the simple ways acquaintances become friends and a parish becomes a family. Whether you would like to host a Sunday coffee hour, bring a dish to the monthly Agape Meal, take dinner to a family who needs one, or ask for a little help yourself, the next step is here.

Every Sunday

Sunday coffee hour

Stay awhile.

Every Sunday after the Divine Liturgy, everyone is invited to the parish hall for coffee, food and fellowship. Visitors are included in that — you need bring nothing at all, and you may leave whenever you need to.

Coffee hour is where we welcome people who have come for the first time, catch up with friends, meet the ones we have not met yet, and spend an unhurried hour together as a parish family.

Parish households help make it possible by taking turns to sponsor the Sunday coffee hour. Once a month the whole parish would bring it together instead — see the Agape Meal below.

What does a coffee hour host provide?

The parish has not published what a host is asked to bring, what the kitchen already provides, or what it usually costs, so nothing is stated here. Those are the details this section is waiting for, and they are the ones that decide whether somebody who has never done it before feels able to offer.

Until they are confirmed, the parish office can tell you what is involved and put you in touch with someone who has hosted before.

Once a month

The Agape Meal

Come to the table.

View upcoming parish events →
Photograph to come A long table in the parish hall — food being passed, several generations sitting together

Agape is the Greek word for self-giving love.

From the earliest days of the Church, Christians gathered not only for prayer and the Eucharist but around a common table, at meals known as agape — love feasts. The Eucharist and the community meal became distinct very early, by the end of the first century or the beginning of the second. What has continued ever since is the plainer thing underneath: that sitting down to eat together is how a household knows itself to be one.

So this is not an attempt to put back together something the Church deliberately separated, and it asks nothing of anybody at the Liturgy. It is a parish supper that remembers where the habit came from.

Once a month, we would like to make our Sunday fellowship especially intentional by gathering for a full parish Agape Meal after the Divine Liturgy. Rather than one household carrying the whole of coffee hour, everyone who is able brings something to share.

Bring a favourite dish.

Bring your family.

Sit with someone you do not know yet.

Welcome the person who came for the first time.

Stay a little longer.

There is room at the table for you.

Like our spiritual ancestors in the Church of the First Century, we now come to an Agape Meal.

His Eminence Archbishop Elpidophoros of AmericaFollowing the Divine Liturgy at Holy Trinity Greek Orthodox Cathedral, New Orleans · 16 March 2025

Why an Agape Meal?

Sunday fellowship can easily become something we pass through on the way to the car. Once a month, we would like to slow it down.

A shared meal makes room for the things a Sunday morning otherwise leaves out: eating together rather than standing with a cup, welcoming a visitor properly, letting the children meet the rest of the parish, and friendships across generations that the Liturgy itself gives no occasion to form.

The aim is not a larger coffee hour. The aim is fellowship.

Proposed — not yet in place

The littlest seats at the table

Children are part of the parish family too. A Kids' Corner during fellowship — books, colouring, quiet play, and now and then something to make that belongs to the saint or the feast of the day — would make it easier for families to stay, eat, and be together.

Parents would stay responsible for their own children. The corner is not childcare, and is not meant to take anyone's children off their hands; it is meant to make an hour at the table possible with them.

The Kids' Corner does not exist yet. It is described here as a proposal, alongside the people it would need — see “There is a place for you” below.

When someone needs a hand

Care for one another

There are seasons when a home-cooked meal means more than we realise.

When someone in our parish welcomes a new baby, goes in for surgery, is unwell or recovering, has lost someone they love, or simply needs a little extra support for a while, the parish family can help carry it. Offering to cook is one way. Letting someone else cook for you is another, and it is just as much a part of this.

How can our parish family help?

Meals are only one of the ways we look after one another. If you are facing something and are not sure whom to ask, please reach out anyway.

You do not need to know which ministry handles it, or which person. Tell the office what is going on and they will find the right next step. If what you need is a conversation with Fr. John, the office will arrange it.
